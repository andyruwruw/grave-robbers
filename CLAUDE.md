# Graveyard (halloween-display)

Top-down pixelated voxel horror party game. The TV shows the world; phones are the controllers. Sibling of `../home-karaoke` in spirit, but real-time, so it uses WebSockets instead of polling. Game design and milestones live in `docs/design.md`.

## Layout

npm workspaces, one install at the root.

- `shared/`: the map editor's Mix brush (`blend.ts`: a `Blend` of percentages laid out by `blendTile` on a fixed blue-noise rank per tile, so repainting is a no-op; `graveFits`; tiles remember their mix as `BlendAreas`, kept by `World`, in saves, presets, and the world message, for the Pick tool, which falls back to `measureBlend`), the TV's map editor's edits and palette (`map-edit.ts`: `MapEdit`, applied by `World.edit`: brush, `mix` and `scatter` for paint, erase also deletes ground items, and placed burning lights get `EDITOR_LIGHT_FUEL` (as do a preset's); the TV's editor view (`setFlatLight`, `setMonstersHidden`, `setXray` leaving scenery out of the build while shaping land, and `pickTiles`, which traces the view ray through the terrain and lists the tiles it runs into front to back for the scroll wheel; the brush is round, `brushTiles`/`brushRadius`, `level` edits take a `from` so a stroke only moves one layer, the Stairs tool previews its flight with `setStairsGhost` from `stairsOptions` (on the tile, or at the foot of a wall below it), cycled by right click and placed with a `dir`, and `setTopView` tilts the camera nearly straight down while editing) is display-only; the hub resends the whole map after each batch, and `save-preset` writes `server/presets/<slug>.json` via `savePreset`, `PRESET_DIR` overriding the folder), the knobs for random maps (`map-gen.ts`: `MapGenSettings`, sent with `reset-world` and cleaned by `cleanMapGen`), monster stats and attacks (every attack winds up, `windup`, aiming at its target the whole time, then lands by its `ATTACK_STYLE`: a melee hitbox, an arrow, a lobbed potion, or a pounce; `isShambler` kinds walk like zombies, `isShadowWalker` kinds like vampires, `isZombie` is what carries junk and armor), spawn pacing, and blood moon timings (`monsters.ts`), hidden player and world levels (`levels.ts`: `playerLevel` from best weapon DPS, plate and shield, tree depth points, and online seconds; `worldLevelTarget` 70% strongest, 30% the rest; and what a world level does to the horde: counts, spawn weights, junk and armor chances, health, damage, and rewards; `Game.advanceLevels` eases `worldLevel` (plus the Game tab's `GameSettings.levelBoost`) and hands it to `Horde.level`, applied as monsters spawn; the TV's Overview shows them via `ServerStats`; `Horde.attack` starts a windup, `windUp` keeps the aim on its target, `strike` lands it (hostile shots go through the `shoot` hook as a `HostileShot`; potions burst in `Game.splash`), `leap` flies a ghoul's pounce, `circle` runs it around its prey, `swipe` starts a witch's broom sweep, `shadowStep` moves a demon behind its target, `look` works out sightings once a tick around each player (a facing cone plus `NEAR_SIGHT`, only within `SIGHT_SEARCH`); witches' puddles live in `Game.puddles` (drawn by `puddles.ts`); brute fists and witch brooms leave a faint `trail` the renderer adds to the scene; the sound director times each attack's hit sound to its windup and growls brutes and ghouls while they hunt; the TV models each kind in `monster-avatar.ts` (`buildSkeleton`, `buildWitch`, `buildGhoul`, `buildDemon` and their `animate*`, timed by `attackPhase`) and have their own model and animation, `buildBrute`/`animateBrute`, whose slams bump `impactCount` for the renderer's dust and shake, and whose `quake` shakes nearby trees: `buildGraveyard` bakes trees into their own mesh with each box's tree base as `aSwayBase`, and `tree-sway.ts` patches its vertex shader to push boxes sideways by height near the strongest shake sources, no rebuild), gear stats (weapon families and perks, one- or two-handed, shields that only block while held up, armor by material with speed, steadiness, and thorns, the innate wood axe and hand torch), recipes (arrows and bolts), slots, loot rules, the junk weapons some zombies carry and drop (never craftable), and `damageTo` (slays, holy weapons vs vampires, vampires shrugging off blood weapons) (`gear.ts`), wire protocol (`protocol.ts`), the mutable `WorldMap` tile grid plus a fixed terrain level per tile and everything height (`base`, ramps via `rampUp`/`top`, `elevationAt`, `canStep`: up at most `STEP_UP`, down any drop, and `roundTrip` reachability), `facingTile`, tick rate (`world.ts`), player constants (`players.ts`), candy and blood counters, bag materials, piles' goods, and gatherables (`items.ts`), the build catalog, light definitions, stations that turn materials into others, and the numbers for the altar, blood sentry, bone digger, and silver bell (`structures.ts`; every structure kind is also a `Tile`), the skill tree (`tree.ts`: a node per piece of gear and structure, how each unlocks (start, blueprint, or an upstream node's level); each player has their own, held by `Game` and kept through death, candy upgrade costs, and what levels do to stats). Raw TS, imported as `@halloween/shared/<file>` by both sides. No barrel.
- `server/`: Express + `ws`, run by `tsx`. `game/` is the authoritative simulation (knows nothing about sockets), split into `Game` (players, input, actions, combat: health, melee with the equipped weapon, crossbow bolts and blood shots (blunderbuss and sentries, which never hit players), the ritual knife's health cost (and the slow healing its kills give), no regeneration (campfires mend, tonics heal when drunk from the bag, and nothing heals damage from the last `FRESH_WOUND_SECONDS`, sent as `wounded`), shield and armor, offerings at sacrificial altars (and double blood from deaths near one), knockback, death (the bag spills, equipped gear stays, half the candy and blood is lost) and respawn; leaving keeps belongings, tree, and id by name for a rejoin (in memory only); gear: grab, equip, craft, wear, loot only from chests, rarely pumpkins, and zombies' junk; `moveItem` moves, stacks, swaps, and equips between bag and equipped slots; built lights come down in one hit as a crate; crafted structures and gathered materials go into the bag as stacks (structures are placed from there, costs paid from it); each player's own skill tree, upgraded with candy and blood at a research table; digging up graves for candy, wood, gear, and blueprints (what's dug, robbed, opened, or killed goes straight to whoever did it via `award`, a pile only for what won't fit; gear and structures on the ground are picked up into the bag by walking over them, `autoGrab`, never equipped, and what you just dropped waits a moment), and robbing statues, crypts, and mausoleums (`ROBBABLE`: they stay standing, robbed for `ROB_REFILL`, a crypt or mausoleum as a whole); trees, rocks, and hedges pay out all at once when they come down; lamp posts break into a lantern) and `World` (`world.ts`: tiles, partly gathered nodes and part-dug graves, rotting stumps and rock regrowth, dug-up mounds that a new gravestone rises from, stations pulling inputs from piles in reach into a buffer, working through their queue, and handing what they make to a station in reach that takes it (else dropping it beside them), bone diggers gathering from a tree or rock beside them, coffins (respawn points, owned by their builder's player id), blood sentries' loaded shots, silver bells, growing trees, pumpkins, and chests back toward fixed targets (only on grass, never where a flood fill says it would cut off a reachable tile), ground items, which are either resource piles picked up by walking over them or a single bag item (gear or a stack of structures) picked up with Grab; everything that's saved), plus `Horde` (`horde.ts`: spawning and the zombie, vampire, and ghost AI (ghosts lob slow ectoplasm through walls via the `shoot` hook: hostile shots that slow the player they hit, shown hazy via `PlayerState.slowed`; a ghost that sees someone hunts them, hovering `GHOST_KEEP` off with an angry face; weapons hit ghosts too, via `Horde.targets`, for a little damage and `GHOST_KNOCKBACK` times the knockback); monsters aren't saved, `Game` runs the blood moon timer, which only counts while someone is connected, and a few zombies wander even with nobody playing; idle zombies path to a tile a few steps off, some (`lure`) to hot spots of a player heat map (where players have been, fading) or a grave heat map; zombies take no fall damage) on top of `pathing.ts` (8-way A*/Dijkstra over tiles, never cutting corners), and the seeded 56x34 generator (`world-gen.ts`, shaped by `MapGenSettings`: crypt, winding paths, landmarks per quadrant (a round walled chapel and a round well plaza in random sizes, `COURTYARD_SIZES`, one to three separate mausoleums) including the hill with the watchtower, broad terraces and a hollow (nothing narrower than 3 tiles) with stairs where paths cross, more stairs until every tile is reachable there and back, hedged plots, forest edge, groves). Collision is height-aware (`collision.ts` `walkerBlocked`; `moveWithCollision` slides a mover heading straight past a corner it barely clips around it; a walker stands as high as the ground under any part of it; trees are `PASSABLE`, slowing whoever is within `TRUNK_RADIUS` of a tree's center (`inTrunk`) to `TREE_SLOW` for players and `MONSTER_TREE_SLOW` for zombies and vampires), and combat checks height (reach up and down, high-ground bonus, bolt height, fall damage). `net/connection-hub.ts` routes socket messages into it; displays get the full map on hello, then tile deltas, players, monsters, items, and one-off events after each tick (30Hz; `server.ts` wakes several times per tick and runs however many fixed steps real time calls for, since Windows timers are too coarse for a plain 33ms interval). A phone's Panic (`Game.panic`) steers its player along a path away from monsters for `PANIC_SECONDS`, then back home. Building goes on `placeTile` (the facing tile, pushed past the builder if they'd overlap it), the same tile the TV previews; crafting takes a `count`. Phones get their candy and blood (any gain pops a "+6 candy" toast with the icon at the top, `pickup-toasts.tsx`), gear and bag (materials live in the bag as stacks), their own skill tree (and whether they stand by a research table; the Tree panel upgrades), what Grab would pick up, and vitals (hp, protection, death info) when they change.
- `client/`: one Vite + React app. `/` is the phone controller, `/tv` is the display, `/admin` is the TV's sidebar controls on their own page. The display's three.js scene lives in plain TS classes under `pages/display/renderer/`, fed snapshots directly (not through React state). Any tile change rebuilds the world voxels; `build-graveyard.ts` has one painter per tile and decoration is seeded per tile so rebuilds don't reshuffle the rest. Something that just grew is left out of the build and drawn on its own by `buildTile` while it rises (trees, chests, gravestones from a mound) or swells (pumpkins), and a dug-up gravestone is drawn on its own while it shudders down into its mound, then the world is rebuilt with it in place. The camera fits the whole map and `buildOutskirts` fills the rest of the screen with woods beyond the fence (rebuilt when a resize needs more), lit by plain ambient since the light map only covers the map. Non-ground tiles stand on a floor guessed from their neighbors; trees side by side on the same level get undergrowth between them (`shrubBetween`, painted after the tiles), which sinks into the ground (`sinkShrub`) when either tree falls; each tile's contents stand on its terrain level, raised tiles get a rough stone retaining wall with a broken capstone rim on each edge that drops (and a shadow line at its foot), grass is lighter the higher it is, stairs get pale treads and side walls, and avatars, piles, and markers follow `WorldMap.elevationAt` (ramps rise smoothly). Nothing has a roof. The watchtower is a ruin whose upper walls are a separate model (`buildTower`, each wall column crumbled to its own height) whose front half hides while a player is inside. There are no PointLights: `light-map.ts` rebuilds a small quantized texture each frame from every light (pumpkins, burning torches/campfires, lanterns, a display-only glow around ghosts; the blood moon tints its ambient) and patched Lambert materials multiply by it, so darkness costs the same however many lights exist. Glowing Basic materials (flames, pumpkin faces, build ghosts) stay bright. Lights flicker slowly, each on its own rhythm, and the light map stores levels on a square-root curve so the dim ambient keeps fine steps. `fog.ts` simulates a density grid (3 cells per tile) that settles toward a seeded resting shape, never enters tall tiles, and flows around them: walkers push fog into a ring around themselves (only to cells reachable in a straight line) and drag it along, lights thin it, ghosts thicken it. Three stacked, dithered sheets render it, lit by the same light map; only their fine texture creeps. `gear-models.ts` builds each piece of gear from boxes, shared by the avatars' hands, `item-piles.ts` (gear lies flat with a twinkling glint), and `item-models.ts`, which draws the phone's item icons (gear, the lantern, material heaps, candy and blood, and structures via `buildTile`) once each into cached images with one small offscreen renderer, plus a ring of turning frames per item for the details dialog (`itemSpinUrls`). `gore.ts` keeps a pool of blood splats that dry away (and pumpkin seeds that last a moment) and tears dead monsters apart: their parts move into the scene on pivots and are thrown along the killing blow, bounce, rest, and sink. `pickup-popups.ts` pops a small spinning model of whatever someone picks up (the server's `candy` and `pickup` events: piles, wood and stone gathered, blood from an offering; built with `item-models.ts`'s models on glowing materials) over them, `altar-gauges.ts` fills a glowing trough along each lit altar's top as its offering's glow runs down, and `health-bars.ts` puts a small DOM health bar over any tile or monster that survives a hit (hit events and `MonsterState` carry `health`, 0 to 1), fading a moment after the last hit. Tiles that break (structures, pumpkins, chests, felled trees, mined rocks and graves) go the same way: `buildTilePieces` repaints the tile's boxes as loose meshes, cut into small chunks, just before the tile change is applied. `post-effects.ts` runs an EffectComposer at the same low resolution (bloom, then one grade pass for saturation plus focus and chromatic aberration, both scaling with distance from the nearest living player) at fixed default strengths. The TV shows only the map, kill feed, and a small faded join QR; the sidebar (`control-panel.tsx`, shared with `/admin`, which joins as a display with `admin: true`; its music controls go to the TV as `tv-control` and the TV reports `tv-status` back, both relayed by the server; only one TV page plays music: the server hands it to the newest (`music-owner`; the others go quiet and offer Play music here, `claim-music`) and relays only that page's `tv-status`) slides in from a chevron that appears when hovering the top-right corner and has three tabs: Overview (a short roster plus stats: server TPS and tick time from the state message, the renderer's `fps`), Music (including Incognito, on when the TV page opens: `incognito-screen.tsx` shows the album art, title, and artist over the live graveyard, hides the QR and kill feed, mutes SFX, and fades away when a player id newly shows up connected), and Game. The Game tab holds testing controls and reset: summon a blood moon, kill every monster (`Horde.killAll`), switch `GameSettings` (aggressive monsters, the test player, All unlocks and Free crafting (applied per player by `Game.treeOf`, which hands phones a `TreeState` with `all`/`free` set), lights off, daytime (only the TV's look: `LightMap.setSky`, fog, sky light, and focus ease toward day), and the TV mouse's two tricks; held by `Game`, sent back in every state message, not saved), and spawn things or monsters anywhere on the map (`Game.spawn`: `World.spawnTile`, `Horde.summon`). On the TV's map the mouse (`use-tv-mouse.ts`, picking with `WorldRenderer.entityAt`/`groundAt`) lifts a player or monster (`tv-lift`: `Game.lift` freezes it where the mouse holds it, shown raised and swinging like a rag doll from the top of its head (`dangle.ts`, plus each avatar's `flail`); letting go lands it on the nearest open spot) and holding the right button carries `CURSOR_LIGHT` (`tv-light`: a real light for the horde too, sent back as `cursorLight`). Clicking outside the open sidebar closes it. The test player is `game/test-player.ts`, a bot that joins like a phone and drives the same intents (`setInput`, `setAim`, `setAction`, build).

Audio lives in `client/src/audio/`: `synth.ts` (WebAudio recipes for every `SfxName`), `sfx.ts` (one engine per page, boosted by `SFX_BOOST` through a limiter; a `sfx/<name>.mp3` from the server's media overrides a recipe; it also streams the TV's ambience loop at its own volume), `media.ts` (fetches the media manifest once), `music-player.ts` (keeps its own queue: what's queued on the linked Spotify account first, read from `/me/player/queue` and in its order, then the chosen playlist shuffled by the player with custom songs shuffled in by the two sources' weights; the panel can move or remove the player's own upcoming songs; plays Spotify tracks one at a time on the SDK device, fading each out and moving on just before its end unless the account has something queued, and pauses Spotify for custom songs; titles, artists, and art from Spotify or ID3 via `id3.ts`, fetched by Range; with ducking; the panel's choices are `AudioSettings` in `shared/src/audio.ts`), `song-player.ts` (plays a song's parts in step; only one song exists at a time, tracked on `globalThis` so songs from before a hot reload are stopped too, and edits to either player file reload the page; each eased toward a level from the game's mood, which `sound-director.ts` sets: fights bring in drums and melody). and `spotify.ts`. Big media isn't bundled: `server/media/` (`music/` single mp3s or part folders with `cover.jpg` and an optional `meta.json` title and artist that beat the ID3 tags, `sfx/`, `ambience/`) is served by the server's `net/media.ts` at `/media` (Vite proxies it), with a manifest at `/api/media` read fresh per request. The TV's `sound-director.ts` turns snapshots, events, and tile changes into panned SFX; phones get a `sounds` message for their own actions and derive hurt/death/respawn from vitals. The server's `net/spotify.ts` does the OAuth code flow and keeps the refresh token in `server/data/spotify.json` (or takes it from `SPOTIFY_REFRESH_TOKEN`, which wins); `server/.env` is loaded by the server scripts; the TV only gets short-lived access tokens from `/api/spotify/token`. Vite proxies `/api` and `/auth` to the server.

## Running

- `npm run dev`: server on :3021, Vite on :5181 (proxies `/ws` to the server, `host: true` for LAN phones). Open `http://localhost:5181/tv` on the TV machine; the QR swaps in the server's LAN IP.
- Stairs can be turned (`WorldMap.ramps`, `setRamp`, `encodeRamps`; 0 lets `rampUp` choose), sent as `ramps` with the world message and kept in saves and presets. Presets may hold built structures; a world started from one gives them fresh state.
- `npm run dev:fresh`: deletes the world save first. The TV's Game tab also starts one: tick the confirm box, then press New world. New worlds start from a map preset (`server/presets/<id>.json`: seed, tiles, levels; optional `view`, the part of the map the TV frames (`WorldRenderer` aims and fits the camera to it, `World.inView` keeps Game tab spawns inside it), and `quiet`, which starts the world with `GameSettings.spawning` off; `sandbox` is a fenced 10x10 test arena using both; `WORLD_PRESET`, default `king-of-the-hill`) unless the TV picks a random map; `World` takes the preset, `world-store.ts` lists and loads them, and `display-welcome` sends the list.
- `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build` (client), `npm start` (server serves `client/dist` if built).

## Identity

Phones join with name + color and get a secret token, kept in localStorage. On reconnect the phone sends it in `hello-controller` and resumes the same player. A player whose phone drops (put down, screen off) stays in the game slumped where they stood, still hunted and killable, until the phone comes back or they press Leave. Whoever leaves keeps their belongings, tree, and id, handed back when someone joins under the same name (case-insensitive). Players are in memory only; a server restart sends phones back to the join form (name prefilled).

## Persistence

The world (seed, tiles, terrain levels, structure HP/fuel/floor, game clock, node yields and dig progress, stump timers, mound refill timers, unlocked blueprints, tree target, ground items) is saved to `server/data/world.json` (gitignored) on start, every 30s, after a reset, and on SIGINT/SIGTERM. A save with an old `version` or a size that doesn't match `MAP_WIDTH x MAP_HEIGHT` is ignored and a new world is generated.

## Conventions

- frontend-bootstrap, `glass` theme (`.claude/arw-frontend.json`), dark-only (`class="dark"` on `<html>`). Tailwind v3 with `.cjs` configs. UI chrome uses token utilities only; game colors (player palette, voxel colors) are game data and live in `shared/` or the renderer.
- kebab-case files, `// Packages` / `// Local Imports` / `// Types` import groups, JSDoc on components and hooks, single-quoted JSX attributes.
- Server is authoritative: clients send intent (joystick vector, Action down/up, drop), never positions. Everything acts on the facing tile.
