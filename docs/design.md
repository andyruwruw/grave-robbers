# Graveyard design

Decisions from the design interview. The source of truth for what we're building; update it when a decision changes.

## Format and world

1. **Session model:** persistent drop-in world. No rounds, no lobby.
2. **Core loop:** fresh-start characters in a one-screen world that keeps running. Gather, build, survive. Death means starting over.
3. **Building:** gather wood and stone, place structures one tile at a time. The map is server-mutable and broadcast.
4. **Map:** 56x34 tiles, fixed camera, whole map always visible. Max 8 players. Seeded per fresh world: the crypt in a cobbled courtyard at the center, four winding main paths (cobbled near the middle) with two spawns each, a thick, uneven forest edge with clearings, groves dotted over the middle, hedged grave plots with gaps, lamp posts along the paths, and one landmark per quadrant: a ruined chapel (broken walls, statue at the altar), a hill with stairs, corner lamps, and a watchtower on top, a row of mausoleums, or a well plaza (the hill is retried elsewhere if its quadrant is too cramped). Trees come in dead, pine, and autumn variants; hedges can be chopped for wood.
    - **Terrain levels:** every tile has a level: sunken hollows (-1), the ground (0), terraces (1), higher terrace cores (2), and the tower's floors (up to 3), each 1.5 high. The generator raises four broad terraces (some with a higher core) and sinks one hollow away from the crypt, the landmarks, and the spawns, then flattens anything narrower than 3 tiles so every level change is a wide shelf, never a finger or a one-tile bump; paths crossing a level change turn into stone stairs, and more stairs are added until every open tile can be reached from the spawns and left again (pockets that still can't become trees). Every drop is a rough dark brown stone retaining wall (uneven stones, the odd one missing, rubble at the foot) with a broken rim of capstones at uneven heights along the top and a dark shadow line at its foot; grass gets lighter the higher it is and darker in hollows; stairs have pale treads over dark risers and a stepped stone wall up each open side.
    - **Moving between levels:** walkers can step up at most 0.4, so only stairs and built steps climb a level, but they can always drop off an edge. Everyone stands on the highest ground under any part of them, so they only drop once clear of the edge. Monsters path with the same rule.
    - **No roofs:** buildings are open ruins seen from above: the mausoleums' tops are broken, the crypt's doorways are open (no arches), and the well has no cover.
    - **Watchtower:** a 5x5 roofless ruined stone tower on the hill (its walls crumble to uneven heights, lower on the side facing the camera, and the pillar is a broken stump): a doorway toward the map center, a pillar in the middle, and stairs spiraling up around it to a landing and then the top floor, three floors in all. Each flight sits mid-side with a corner landing at its foot. While a player is inside, the TV cuts away the front of its walls so they can be seen climbing; a candle on the pillar lights it.
5. **Persistence:** the world (tiles, structures with HP and fuel, dropped items, seed, leaderboard) is saved to JSON every 30s and on shutdown. Players and monsters are not saved. Time freezes while the server is off. Reset with `npm run dev:fresh` or a long-press on the TV.

## Monsters and pacing

6. **Setting:** always night. Zombies, vampires, ghosts.
7. **Pacing:** zombies and vampires trickle in (one every 4.5s) up to 2 plus 4 per living player (at most 48), at dark tiles near the map edge at least 10 tiles from anyone. A blood moon rises after 8 to 12 minutes of play (that clock only runs while someone is connected; it is not saved) and lasts 90 seconds, fading in and out over about 8 seconds. The TV sidebar can summon one. During it, the cap rises to 2 plus 7 per player, spawns come every second, and the TV turns red. Ghosts spawn on their own clock: one per 3 burning built lights (one per 2 under a blood moon), at most one every 20s. Monsters ignore players under spawn protection and phones that have disconnected. With nobody playing, 10 zombies keep wandering (all out from the moment the server starts) so the TV is never empty (they stay when people join). Difficulty climbs with the players (see 10b). The Game tab's Natural spawning switch (`GameSettings.spawning`) stops all of it (trickle, idle zombies, ghosts, the blood moon clock, and trees, pumpkins, and chests growing back); a preset with `quiet: true` (the Sandbox, a fenced 10x10 arena) starts its worlds with it off, and its `view` makes the TV frame just that part of the map and keeps the Game tab's spawns inside it.
10b. **Player and world levels** (`shared/src/levels.ts`, hidden; the TV's Overview shows them, phones never do): a player's level is offense (5 levels per doubling of their best weapon's damage per second over the wood axe's 6, upgrades included, ranged x1.3, health-costing x0.7) plus defense (5 per doubling of effective health from plate and half a shield's guard) plus tree points / 10 (each node is worth its depth, 1 for a start node, 2 for a blueprint, +1 per step down an upgrade chain, per level and once more for a found blueprint) plus a level per 8 minutes connected (at most 8; kept on leave like the tree). The world level's target is 70% the strongest online player plus 30% the average of the rest; it climbs 1 level per 15s and falls 1 per 60s, and stays put while nobody is connected. At world level L: +0.2L monsters per player, spawns 1 + 0.04L times as often (cap 60), vampire weight x(1 + 0.04L), brutes from L 3 (weight 0.025 per level, up to 0.4 against zombies' 0.55), junk on 12% + 2% per level of zombies and brutes (up to 50%), armor on 4% per level past 2 (up to 60%; helmet takes 75% of each hit, plate 55%, plates from L 9 up to 60% of the armored), new monsters' health x(1 + 0.04L) and damage x(1 + 0.03L), and their blood x(1 + 0.05L). Armored kills leave iron (helmet 30%, plate 80%).
The Game tab's World level boost (`GameSettings.levelBoost`, 0 to 60 in steps of 5) adds to the target and moves the world level at once when changed.
10d. **Skeletons, witches, ghouls** walk like zombies (`isShambler`: chase, shamble, chew walls). Skeleton: 9 HP, speed 1.9, shoots from 6.5 tiles, backs away inside 3, 2 damage, 4 bones and no blood. Witch: 14 HP, speed 1.5, lobs from 6, backs away inside 3.5; the potion does 1.5 and leaves a purple puddle (`PUDDLE_SECONDS` 5, radius 1.2) that does `PUDDLE_DAMAGE` 1 a second and slows anyone in it (`Game.puddles`, sent as `PuddleState`); within 1.3 tiles she swings her broom instead (`WITCH_SWIPE`: 0.4s windup, 1.7-radian sweep, 1.5 damage, 2.8-tile knockback, 2s slow, 1.6s cooldown, counted in `MonsterState.swipes`). 4 blood and 1 bone. Ghoul: 8 HP, speed 4.4, circles its prey `GHOUL_ORBIT` (3) tiles out (within 7, switching direction every few seconds or on bumping into something) until it can pounce from 2 to 3.6 tiles: a 0.5s crouch whose aim locks 0.3s before the leap (`lock`), then `GHOUL_LEAP` 3.6 tiles in 0.32s; 2 damage, cooldown 2s. 2 blood and 2 bones. **Demons** move like vampires (`isShadowWalker`: dark tiles only), 22 HP, speed 2.6, sight 12; from the dark they lunge (`ATTACK_STYLE` `lunge`) at anyone within 3.6 tiles, lit or not: a 0.45s windup whose aim locks 0.12s before, then a dash just past the target (at most `DEMON_LUNGE` 4 tiles in 0.24s) raking the first player it touches for 3.5, cooldown 1.4s; landing in light, it heads back to the dark. While hunting someone more than 3 tiles off, every 5s (give or take a third) one steps through the shadows to a dark tile 1.7 behind its target's back (a `blink` event: a gaseous `shadow-step` whoosh at both ends; `MonsterState.blinks` makes the TV jump it there in black smoke and it pulls itself up to full height). On the TV it's a see-through shadow ghost that crawls on its arms with three smoke trails rippling behind, at 65% opacity, 25% while stalking someone, solid while attacking. At most 2 demons at once. 8 blood, 1 bone. Spawn weights by world level: skeletons from 2, ghouls from 4, witches from 6, demons from 10 (see `spawnWeights`).
10c. **Brutes:** zombie AI, 30 HP, speed 1.15, one swing per 1.3s that winds up for 0.65s (shown as fists raised overhead) and then lands on whoever is still within reach + 0.25 and within 1.1 radians of its aim: 3.5 damage times the level multiplier, capped at 4.5 (`BRUTE_MAX_HIT`), with a 2.4-tile knockback. 6 damage per bite to structures, take no knockback and flinch at 30%. 6 blood and 3 bones. Built from rounded masses (hip-pivoted legs, belly, barrel chest, shoulder hump, sunken head with tusks, long arms with big fists on springs); a slow, long gait (rolling and shifting its weight onto each planted leg), footfalls drop the body, trees within about 3 tiles shiver with each step and shake hard at a slam (`TreeSway`), the swing raises both fists overhead and slams them down with a crouch, dust, and a screen shake.
7b. **Attacks** (`MonsterDef.windup`, `arc`, `range`, `keep`; `ATTACK_STYLE`): nothing hurts on contact. A monster in reach (or, for shooters and ghouls, within `range` and in sight) starts an attack: it stops, turns every tick to face its target (perfect aim), and after `windup` seconds the attack lands: melee hits everyone within reach + `STRIKE_SLACK` (0.25) and `arc` radians of its facing; a skeleton's arrow flies at `ARROW_SPEED` (9) and stops at walls; a witch's potion is lobbed at `POTION_SPEED` (5.5) to where its target stood and bursts over `SPLASH_RADIUS` (1.1), hurting and slowing like ectoplasm; a ghoul leaps `GHOUL_LEAP` (2.4) tiles in `GHOUL_LEAP_TIME` (0.28s), biting the first player within its reach. `cooldown` counts from the start of one attack to the next. Player hits never cancel an attack (knockback still moves most monsters; brutes take none). Zombie reach 1.1 (hitting 1.35). Ghosts knocked back stop at the fence. Windups: zombie 0.35s (cooldown 0.9), vampire 0.25s (0.7), brute 0.65s (1.3), skeleton 0.7s (1.8), witch 0.9s (2.6), ghoul 0.5s (2), demon 0.45s (1.1).
8. **Zombies:** slow, tanky, path to the nearest player (A*, with built structures costed by their HP), chew through walls, doors, fences, and lights in the way, and go for a scarecrow they can see within 8 tiles first. 10 HP, 2 damage per bite. Spikes hurt monsters too.
9. **Vampires:** faster than players and tougher than zombies, blocked by walls and doors, won't enter lit tiles (the brighter 75% of any pumpkin, lamp post, or burning built light; player lanterns don't count); wait at the edge and dash in when a light dies. 18 HP, 3 damage per strike. Holy weapons wreck them (a stake kills in one hit, crossbow bolts do triple damage); blood weapons (the blunderbuss, blood sentries, the ritual knife) do only a quarter. They leave 6 blood. They hiss when they spot someone and when they strike; zombies snarl when they spot someone, and both cry out when hurt. About 1 zombie in 8 (more as the world level rises) carries junk (a stick, a femur club, or a bent shovel: never better than the wood axe, each with one odd perk), hits 1 harder for it, and drops it when it dies; zombies drop nothing else.
10a. **Sight and wandering:** monsters only notice and attack players they can see: anyone within `NEAR_SIGHT` (5) tiles in any direction, or within their `sight` (zombies 7, vampires 14) inside `SIGHT_CONE` (1.1 radians) either side of where they face. Walls, doors, hedges, statues, ruins, the crypt, and mausoleums block line of sight; trees, gravestones, fences, rocks, and lights don't. `Horde.look` works it out once a tick by searching around each player: only monsters within `SIGHT_SEARCH` (15) tiles are checked, and only those close enough get a line traced. After losing sight they head for where they last saw someone (zombies remember 6s, vampires 4s; being hit counts as being seen), look around, and give up. Otherwise they wander: zombies pick a tile a few steps off and shamble there along a path at their own pace, weaving a little, pausing once there, with the odd stumble (a lurch and a moment to recover); some are drawn toward where players have been lately and some toward clusters of graves; vampires prowl from one dark spot to another. Ghosts meander with a sway.
10. **Ghosts:** light thieves. Drift through walls, and every 3 to 5 seconds lob a slow ball of ectoplasm (also through walls) at anyone within 5 tiles: 1 damage and 45% speed for 2.5s. Otherwise they leave players alone and drift to the nearest burning built light and snuff it after 3s inside it. A player standing in the light scares them off for 4s. Invulnerable.

## Players and combat

11. **PVP:** always on. Inventory drops on death. Structures are unowned.
12. **Death and respawn:** dying is meant to hurt, never a way to heal: the bag spills where you fell, half your candy and blood is gone for good, and only equipped gear stays with you. 5s respawn, phone death screen with time survived and killer, random spawn point (or beside your coffin), 10s spawn protection broken by attacking or building. TV kill feed.
13. **Phone controls:** landscape. Floating joystick on the left; big context Action button (attack, chop, dig, refuel, feed a station) and big Bag, Craft, and Tree buttons down the middle (Bag and Craft replace Action and Grab with their list and a Panic button while open). Action is also an aim stick: dragging it from where the thumb lands (snapped to 8 directions) turns the player to strike that way, behind them included, while the left stick keeps moving them. Everything acts on the tile you face. Craft lists everything that can be made (bolts, gear, structures); what's made goes into the bag. Structures are placed from the Bag: tapping a stack shows a preview on the TV (a dim white outline and see-through shape on the facing tile, light red if it can't go there, with whatever is in the way, a player, monster, pile, or the thing on the tile, outlined light red too), and tapping it again places one. Grab (next to Action) picks up the piece of gear or crate of structures in front of you and shows its icon when there is one. Bag opens the equipped slots and the bag. Holding anything in Craft, the Bag, or the skill tree opens its details: its name, its model turning slowly, labeled stat bars colored by strength (red, amber, green), traits in words, cost, and for held gear how much use is left. Tree opens the skill tree full screen: nodes linked to what they grow from, unseen ones as question marks, a side panel to upgrade the picked node, Back, and Panic (the player runs from monsters within 4 tiles for 2s along a path, then walks back home once nothing is within 3, giving up after 3 more seconds). Candy and blood counters (a wrapped yellow and orange sweet and a blood drop, both 3D) in a corner; candy and blood are dropped from the Bag.
14. **Health:** 10 hearts, and no regeneration. Damage taken in the last 10s can't be healed yet (grey hearts on the phone, a grey end on the TV bar). Healing: rest by a burning campfire (1 heart per 4s within 2 tiles), drink a tonic (+4 hearts, refused when there's nothing it could heal), or kill with the ritual knife (each kill heals 4 hearts over 8s; held back by fresh wounds, wasted at full health). Leaving keeps everything: rejoining under the same name restores your belongings and tree.
15. **Height in combat:** swings and bites reach down 1.8 (a full level) but up only 0.6, so whoever stands higher (a terrace, a platform, the tower) can hit without being hit back; swinging down at a target at least 0.5 lower does +1 damage. Crossbow bolts fly 2 tiles further per level the shooter stands above the ground, and stop at ground rising above them. Being knocked off a drop of at least 0.6 costs 1 heart per level fallen (monsters too).
16. **Melee:** a swing lands 0.22s after the press, when the axe comes down on the TV. Everyone always has a wood axe (used when no weapon is equipped): 3 damage, 0.5s cooldown, hits everything within 1.8 tiles in the half-circle in front (it cleaves through crowds), a full tile of knockback, phone haptic on hit. Other weapons change the reach, arc, damage, and knockback. On the TV the axe is hauled far back, hangs, then whips down with a lunge and crouch, leaving a glowing streak; the impact kicks up dust, hits throw glowing sparks and shake the screen, and struck zombies' heads snap the way they were hit. Zombies rear up and slam down when they attack. Players and walking monsters can't overlap: walking into a zombie shoves it along. Hits spray blood that stains the ground for a while; killed zombies and vampires burst apart, their limbs, head, and torso flung the way the blow pushed, and the pieces lie there a few seconds before sinking away. Anything that breaks (walls, lights, pumpkins, chests, felled trees, mined rocks) bursts into chunks of itself the same way; pumpkins leave seeds for a moment.
17. **Gear:** three equipped slots (weapon, off-hand, armor) and a bag of 16 more, which also holds crafted structures (stacks of up to 99 of one kind; dropped stacks lie as a glinting crate) and materials (stacks of up to 99). Gear comes from the Craft list on the phone or from loot, which only turns up in chests (always one piece), smashed pumpkins (4%), and the junk some zombies carry. Found gear is partly worn. Built lights (torches, lanterns, campfires) come down in one hit and drop as a crate to grab and place again (unless the hit refuels one burning low). Grab puts a piece straight into its slot if that's empty, otherwise in the bag (a full bag leaves it). In the phone's Bag, tapping an item picks it up and tapping another slot moves it there (stacking, swapping, or equipping); tapping it again uses it; a drop slot appears while something is picked up. Each piece wears out (weapons per landed hit, the shield and armor per blocked hit, the torch per second) and breaks at zero. The bag spills on death; equipped gear stays on you. Ground gear glints so it can be found in the dark. The TV shows what each player has equipped.

**Automation:** a station pulls what it needs from piles within 1.6 tiles of its middle (drop wood beside a sawmill and walk away), and hands what it makes straight to another station that close which takes it, so a bone digger beside a rock with a grave sifter next to it turns the rock into silver on its own, and a furnace beside a blood forge (with blood piles dropped by it, or monsters dying near it) turns stone into bloodsteel. Each station holds up to two batches of each input waiting, plus its queue of 5.

19. **Light:** wood is fuel. Action on a light adds 1 wood and resets its timer. Player lanterns are radius 1.5, permanent, too small to repel vampires. Unlit tiles render dark on the TV.
20. **Resources:** trees (5 wood each), rocks (5 stone each), and graves. Planks, iron, silver, and bloodsteel only come out of stations. Bone comes from the dead: zombies leave 1 and vampires 2 with their blood, and graves give 1 to 3 more 60% of the time. Graves (headstones and crosses) are dug up with Action from the tile in front: 3 digs with the wood axe (weapons that chop faster dig faster), then the stone shudders down into the ground and leaves an open mound, and a new gravestone rises from it 3 to 5 minutes later (once nobody is on it and its pile is picked up). A dug-up grave gives 2 to 5 candy, half the time 1 to 3 wood, a 35% chance of a piece of gear, and a 25% chance of a blueprint (placeholder odds until there are loot tables). A blueprint unlocks a random blueprint node of the digger's skill tree (below), and the TV feed says who found what. A felled tree's stump rots away after 2 to 4 minutes. The map keeps itself stocked toward fixed targets, so a server left alone never fills up: trees (as many as the map started with, stumps counting) rise out of the ground beside other trees (mostly toward the middle) every ~12s while short, pumpkins (10) swell up beside paths every ~40s, and up to 3 chests surface beside graves and hedges every ~60s. Nothing grows on a spawn, a person, an item, or anywhere it would cut off a reachable tile. Rocks top up to 20.
    - Pumpkins burst after 2 hits. Built lights take damage like any structure once they're full (hitting one that's burning low with wood still refuels it). Lamp posts are permanent.
    - Chests break open with one hit and spill a piece of gear plus some wood, candy, and sometimes bolts. They glow faintly so they can be found in the dark.
21. **Inventory:** candy and blood are counters with no limit. Everything else you collect (wood, planks, stone, iron, bolts, bone, silver, bloodsteel, tonics) goes into the bag as stacks; the bag is big on purpose, since managing it isn't part of the game. Piles are picked up by walking over them (materials into the bag), gear and structure crates with Grab. Dropping a material stack leaves a pile. On death, bag materials spill as one pile with gear and structure crates scattered around it, and half your candy and blood is lost. Tapping a tonic drinks it. No storage.
22. **Skill tree:** every player has their own, kept through death but not saved (it lasts while the server runs). Every piece of gear and every structure is a node. Branches: weapons, armor, light, fortifications, tables, processing, and occult (the Unlock column and notes above). A node is known from the start, opened by a blueprint, or opened by upgrading the node it grows from to a level. Blueprints a player digs up open nodes in their own tree. Upgrading costs candy and blood (4 and 1, 8 and 3, then 12 and 5; 3 levels per node) and only works standing by a research table, from the phone's Tree panel; candy and blood are for nothing else. Dead zombies leave 2 blood, vampires 3. Each level makes that thing better when that player uses or builds it (structures keep their builder's level): weapons hit 20% harder, gear lasts 25% longer, shields and armor block 0.05 more, structures get 30% more health (new ones), built lights burn 30% longer, spikes hurt 30% more, and stations work half again as fast. The TV feed shows each upgrade and anything it opened.

## Presentation

23. **TV HUD:** sidebar with a small QR, the roster (hearts, weapon, survival time) and the blood moon countdown. Map overlays: kill feed, damage numbers, blood moon splash and red tint. An all-time "Longest night" top 3, saved with the world. Pixel font for HUD text.
24. **Sound:** retro SFX synthesized with WebAudio, each replaceable by an mp3 of the same name. The TV plays world sounds panned by map position; phones play their own player's actions, hurts, and death. Music plays on the TV from bundled mp3s or a Spotify playlist (Web Playback SDK, Premium), chosen in the TV sidebar, and ducks under deaths and the blood moon. The TV keeps its own queue: songs queued on the Spotify account (a phone or a Jam) first, then the playlist shuffled with custom songs shuffled in. An ambience loop plays under everything at its own volume. `/admin` has the TV's controls on a separate page. Songs split into parts mix with the game: all parts with nobody around, a quieter bed while exploring, drums and full melody in a fight.

## Milestones

1. World foundation: 48x30 seeded map, mutable tile grid (full on connect, deltas after), JSON save/load, TV reset.
2. Phone v2 and gathering: landscape layout, Action button, facing tile, resource nodes, inventory, pickup, drops, regrowth.
3. Building and light: Build menu with TV ghost preview, the structures, fuel, darkness via a light-map texture.
4. Combat and death: hearts, melee, knockback, spawn protection, death screen, respawn, kill feed.
5. Monsters: zombies (A*, wall breaking), vampires (light avoidance), ghosts (snuffing), trickle spawner, blood moon.
6. Weapons and gear: Craft list, weapons, torch, shield, plate, durability, bolt projectiles, grave and monster loot, Grab and the bag.
7. HUD polish: roster stats, leaderboard, damage numbers, blood moon splash and tint.

## Weapons, shields, and lights (latest)

- **Weapon tree** (`TREE` in `shared/src/tree.ts`, all "after X" at level 1): Spear (start) leads to Stake and Scythe; Scythe to Battle axe, Short sword, Quarterstaff, Glaive; Battle axe to Flail (then Morning star), Warhammer, Spiked gauntlet; Short sword to Greatsword. Bow (start) leads to Crossbow; Crossbow to Repeating crossbow and Nightmare blunderbuss (the old blood blunderbuss, renamed and moved); blunderbuss to Musket and the Blood sentry. Arrows (4 per wood) and bolts (3 per wood) are always craftable. Shield is a start node; Plate follows it at level 2.
- **Families and perks** (`WeaponStats.family`, `FAMILY_PERKS`, applied in `weaponStats`): every level +20% damage, plus pole +0.3 reach, blade arc cosine -0.12, heavy +25% knockback, fist 12% faster swings, ranged +20% chance per level a shot spares its ammo (`thrift`).
